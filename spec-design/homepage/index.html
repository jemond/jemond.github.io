Books I've read

An annotated list of everything I have read since 2017. Newest first.

  1. 251Tusculan Disputations, by Cicero — A tough read given the style differences of Latin and 2,000 years ago, but a mind bender. I read this as it is one of the most commonly cited books amongst all of our Founding Fathers.
  2. 250The Pursuit of Happiness, by Jeffrey Rosen — Meanders a bit, and thin at times, but useful to help understand the thinking of our founders that created the most remarkable nation in history.
  3. 249Breakneck: China's Quest to Engineer the Future, by Dan Wang — A fascinating book about China and America by a person that doesn't have a complete picture of either.
  4. 248The Origin of Wealth, by Eric D. Beinhocker — A book that makes everything fall into place. Nails the insight, not the prose.
  5. 247Everything Is Tuberculosis, by John Green — An unserious book, by an unserious author, about a serious topic.
  6. 246Careless People, by Sarah Wynn-Williams — A startling and chilling indictment of the moral bankruptcy of Mark Zuckerberg.
  7. 245The Glorious Cause: The American Revolution, 1763–1789, by Robert Middlekauff — If you want to live through the founding era, to breathe it in, to be amongst it, to understand it, read this book. Wonderful.
  8. 244American Midnight, by Adam Hochschild — If you think Trump is a dictator, read this book. You can thank me later.

Abandoned

Books I started but could not finish.

  1. Enzo Ferrari: Power, Politics and the Making of an Automobile Empire — After 150 pages and two serious mentions of supernatural magic in a biography I had to dismiss this as utter drivel.
  2. The Rise of English — Less a book and more a form of punishment.
  3. Fantastic Numbers and Where to Find Them — I thought it was going to be a book about math but it was a meandering unexciting book about physics.
  4. Athens: A Portrait of the City in its Golden Age — Could not get my arms around the prose.
