250 years. How do you measure a nation?

July 4, 2026

Do you measure it in lives given? Roughly 1.3 million Americans have died in this country's wars since 1775 — most of them volunteers, each giving what Lincoln at Gettysburg called the last full measure of their devotion, for an idea larger than all of us.

Do you measure it in prosperity? A $30 trillion economy — larger than China, Germany, and Japan combined — producing roughly a quarter of everything the world makes with 4% of its people.

Do you measure it in what ordinary life became? By almost any material measure, a working person today lives better than kings did for most of human history: more medicine, more mobility, more music, more comfort, more safety, and more second chances.

Tyranny has been the default state of mankind for 6,000 years.

Do you measure it in the triumph of liberty over tyranny? Tyranny has been the default state of mankind for 6,000 years. Liberty is the rare exception, bought and held at terrible cost. America is the greatest of those exceptions, the greatest and most enduring victory of freedom over force the world has ever seen.

Or do you measure it in the mistakes, the ones we name out loud, argue over in public, and keep working to fix? As a former president once said, there is nothing wrong with America that cannot be cured by what is right with America. A country confident enough to audit itself is a rare thing.

Here's what I actually believe: America is not a guarantee. It is not self-executing. Our Constitution is a remarkable thing, but in the end it is a powerless piece of paper. What made this the most successful experiment in human dignity ever attempted is that, generation after generation, enough people decided it was worth cherishing.

The spirit of '76 didn't end in 1776. It's a debt that comes due every generation. Ours included.

E pluribus unum. Out of many, one. Something larger than any of us, handed forward, still undiminished, always unfinished, to our children.

That's something worth remembering as we celebrate 250 years.

Happy Independence Day.
